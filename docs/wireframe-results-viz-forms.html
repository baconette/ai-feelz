Wireframe: individual results visualization — 4 candidate forms

Feature 2 — all four use the same synthetic respondent profile so the comparison isolates form legibility, not data: Healthcare 4/5, Finances 2/5, Home & Personal Life 3.5/5, Media & Culture 5/5 (uneasy↔enthusiastic axis placeholder).

FormStatic contentData-drivenWhere "see individual use case" lives
1 — Radar / spider Axis labels, grid rings Shape/vertex positions per domain score Tap a vertex → expands that domain's use-case list. Satisfies the acceptance criterion, but adds a required interaction most visitors may not discover unprompted.
2 — Spectrum / dial Axis end-labels ("Uneasy"/"Enthusiastic") Needle position, one-line lean label Entirely on-demand via "See domain breakdown," then use-case detail nested a level deeper still. Most glanceable, but pushes the most content behind taps.
3 — Archetype card Icon/avatar shape Headline archetype + one-line summary, both generated from the data Fully on-demand via "See how you rated each domain." Most likely to feel "expressive" per the PRD's language, at the cost of being the most subjective to get right editorially.
4 — Bar chart per subdomain Bar track backgrounds Every bar's length and label Already inline — no tap needed. Flagged risk: this sample shows 4 bars; at full scale (up to 103 use cases) this form likely fails "no additional explanation copy" and "at a glance" outright.

Cross-cutting annotations: none of the four should show a loading spinner — all render from data already computed during the rating session, satisfying the <2s requirement structurally. Forms 1 and 2 (SVG-based) need explicit mobile scaling checks — radar charts compress badly under 320px width; Form 4 needs horizontal scroll or truncated labels at that width. Accessibility flag for later: none of these are screen-reader-legible as drawn — production build needs a text-equivalent data table alongside the visual, not just alt text on the chart.
